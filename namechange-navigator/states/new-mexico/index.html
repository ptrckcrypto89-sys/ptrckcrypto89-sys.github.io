New Mexico marriage name change: government requirements

New Mexico marriage records are issued by county clerks, not the state health department. New Mexico MVD expressly accepts an original or certified marriage license as name-change proof for its driver credentials and for vehicle title/registration owner-name updates. For changes not legally established by the marriage record, use the district-court name-change process. The reviewed MVD name-change guidance does not identify a universal 10-day or 30-day post-marriage driver-license deadline; do not import one from a different state.

1. Obtain the certified marriage record from the county clerk

Contact the county clerk in the county that issued your marriage license and request an official certified copy after the completed license has been recorded. The New Mexico Department of Health confirms it does not issue marriage licenses or certified marriage records. Certified copy costs and delivery methods vary by county; ask that clerk for current fees and eligibility requirements. The MVD lists marriage licenses among documents accepted for married-name correction, but says all submitted documents must be originals or certified copies. When the marriage took place outside New Mexico, get an official certified record from the jurisdiction that registered the marriage.

2. Correct your name with Social Security

Use the Social Security Administration to obtain a corrected Social Security card. Present an original or issuing-agency-certified marriage license/record and the other identity or lawful-status evidence SSA requires. Name correction with SSA is free; eligible people can begin online, while others need to submit documents or attend an appointment. New Mexico's REAL ID guidance advises applicants whose legal names do not match proof documents to apply to SSA for an amended Social Security card. Update your Social Security information before the MVD visit when possible to reduce identity-match problems.

SSA free name change · SSA name evidence · New Mexico REAL ID identity match

3. Visit an MVD field office to correct your New Mexico license or ID

New Mexico's Motor Vehicle Division says its licensing staff require original or certified marriage license, court order or divorce decree when changing a legal name on a driver license, permit or ID. A birth certificate is primarily used to correct an existing record error, not to prove a marriage change. Bring your existing credential and any additional identity, residency or identification-number records required for your standard or REAL ID issuance. State MVD field offices currently run by appointment; some contracted municipal offices permit walk-ins and may add a service charge. A new temporary license is printed, while the final card is mailed.

Fee distinction: The official MVD credential fee schedule lists $18 for a four-year driver license and $34 for an eight-year license; its replacement credential page also lists those amounts for lost/stolen replacements. The agency does not publish a separate marriage-name change fee in the reviewed name-correction instructions, so confirm whether your specific update is assessed at the replacement/renewal level and any municipal surcharge before paying. Do not confuse the first-issue license price with a guaranteed name-correction charge.

4. Update your vehicle title and registration owner name

MVD Chapter 5, Section D specifically addresses a name correction on a vehicle title or registration. Present the original or certified marriage license, court order or divorce decree linking the two names. If a lien exists, the lienholder must acknowledge or agree to the name change before MVD completes the correction. Make sure the same person remains the owner; adding a spouse or transferring ownership is a different title transaction. The MVD's posted base-fee sheet lists $5 for a title change but warns that some municipal offices charge an additional local fee. Confirm the current total and documentation when booking.

5. Update New Mexico voter registration before the 2026 election

New Mexico's Secretary of State instructs voters to check and update voter registration if they changed their name or address. Standard advance voter registration ends 28 days before Election Day; for the November 3, 2026 general election that date was October 6, 2026. But New Mexico separately offers same-day voter registration and existing-record updates at county clerk offices, participating early-voting locations and Election Day polling sites. Applicants must present a physical government/tribal/educational photo ID whose name reasonably matches the updated record, plus a qualifying recent document if its address differs. New Mexico early voting began October 6; additional sites open October 17 and early voting ends October 31. Election Day is November 3. Use the official SOS location tools and confirm county-specific times.

6. Change your U.S. passport if necessary

Use the State Department passport legal-name-change guidance: DS-5504 applies only when its qualifying conditions, including the one-year issuance/name-change window, are met. DS-82 and DS-11 apply to other renewal or in-person cases. Bring or mail the certified name-change document, passport, photo and fees specified for your chosen process. Review travel bookings before sending your passport to be amended.

7. Update payroll, finance, insurance and other accounts

After agency corrections, update payroll and employment taxes, banking, insurance policies, professional credentials, health records, schools and property records. Store the certified marriage evidence securely, and provide official copies only to the agencies or counterparties that request them.

When New Mexico requires a district-court name change

Where marriage documents do not legally establish the requested new name, New Mexico's court self-representation portal provides an adult district-court name-change petition process. The court's published guidance instructs applicants to file the petition and hearing form at the district court clerk, and it lists a $132 filing fee unless a permitted fee-waiver arrangement applies. Court forms include a petition, notice of petition, order and related documents; the signed order does not take effect until filed by the court clerk. These steps are separate from ordinary DMV married-name correction and may vary by court or applicant circumstances.

New Mexico preparation checklist

  1. Order the certified marriage license from the issuing county clerk; the state health office does not issue it.
  2. Update SSA with original or certified legal marriage evidence at no charge.
  3. Bring certified marriage evidence and identity documents to an MVD appointment for corrected driver license or ID.
  4. Change owner names on title/registration as needed; get lienholder acknowledgment and confirm the $5 posted base charge.
  5. Update voter registration or use New Mexico's same-day registration channel with compliant physical photo ID and address evidence.
  6. Review passport eligibility and update payroll, insurance and financial records.
