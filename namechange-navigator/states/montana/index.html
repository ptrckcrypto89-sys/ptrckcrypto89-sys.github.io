Step-by-step Montana agency checklist

1. Get the correct certified marriage document

Montana Department of Public Health and Human Services explains that the Clerk of District Court in the county where the license was purchased is the official custodian authorized to issue certified Montana marriage records. State Vital Records maintains a statewide index but does not issue the marriage certificates used for identity name changes. Ask the issuing county court clerk for a certified marriage record and its fee.

2. Update Social Security first

Follow the SSA legal-name correction instructions and allow time for your updated name to match federal verification. Montana MVD generally specifies at least 24 hours, but its 2026 REAL ID checklist says to allow 48 hours; applicants with REAL ID should follow that stricter guidance.

3. Visit the Motor Vehicle Division in person

Schedule an in-person appointment at an MVD Driver Exam Station. Bring current driver-license or accepted identity proof plus an original issuing-government-certified marriage certificate or another qualifying legal-name document. If you have changed names more than once, you may need documents connecting every change. Your new SSA record must match the name MVD is asked to print.

MVD lists a $10.30 replacement noncommercial driver-license / ID-card charge. Changing your name on a credential is not equivalent to replacing a lost credential online. Verify current office and transaction requirements before visiting.

4. Correct any Montana vehicle title

The March 2026 Montana Title Manual explains that a title holder who changes legal name through marriage need not sign off ownership as seller. First update the Montana driver license; then submit Application for Replacement Certificate of Title (MV7) with the associated title fee. If a lienholder holds the title, confirm procedures with MVD and the lender. Fees listed in the manual may incur a 3% administrative charge.

5. Update voter registration with your county

Vote Montana says residents who have changed names should fill out an updated voter-registration application and return it to their county election office. Check election-specific deadlines and verification before assuming another agency corrected your voter record.
