Michigan marriage name change: state and federal steps

Michigan's Secretary of State accepts an official certified marriage certificate as evidence for correcting a married legal surname, provided your identity and the Social Security record match. A separate court-ordered name change is a different legal process, needed for some changes not established by marriage evidence. Certified vital-record costs and motor-vehicle correction costs are not the same transaction.

1. Obtain an official Michigan certified marriage certificate

Order from the Michigan Department of Health and Human Services (state vital records) or the county clerk that issued and recorded the marriage license. Michigan's state vital-record office currently charges $34 for a standard record search including one certified copy or no-find letter; the official marriage application lists $16 per additional certified copy ordered together and an additional $12 for expedited processing if selected. Online requests through its approved service can add a $14 processing fee. The request typically needs both spouses' names, county/date of marriage, the requester’s identification, and payment. The search fee is generally nonrefundable if no record exists. If the marriage was recorded outside Michigan, obtain the certified record from that jurisdiction instead.

2. Change your Social Security name record before visiting SOS

The Michigan Secretary of State electronically verifies your name and Social Security number and cannot process a new license/ID name when your SSA record still reflects the former name. Apply for a corrected card directly with the Social Security Administration using original or issuing-agency-certified marriage evidence and other required identity or lawful-status documents. Eligible applicants can start online; some applications require Form SS-5 or an office appointment. SSA changes are free. Make sure the new record has been processed before the SOS visit; a pending application alone does not guarantee an identity match.

3. Correct the Michigan driver's license or state ID in person

Michigan Secretary of State's license or ID name correction service requires an office visit; online, self-service station, and mail name-change transactions are unavailable. Bring your existing license/ID, official SSA-name change proof, and at least one document connecting old/new legal names: a certified marriage certificate, divorce decree issued in the U.S., or court order. An applicant with multiple past names may need the complete certificate chain for a REAL ID credential. Michigan's posted name-correction charge is $9 for a driver's license and $10 for a state ID. A name correction is not the same as an enhanced-credential upgrade; confirm any additional fees if you simultaneously change credential class.

Deadline check: The reviewed Michigan SOS name-correction instructions do not state a universal post-marriage 10-day or 30-day deadline. Do not apply unrelated vehicle-sale or address-update deadlines to an adult's marriage-based name correction without statutory support.

4. Obtain a corrected Michigan vehicle title, if you own a vehicle

Michigan provides a specifically documented title name-correction transaction at a Secretary of State office. Bring the original title, vehicle registration, corrected driver's license or official legal-name evidence, and the $15 title-correction fee. All listed owners must attend or an absent owner may complete an Appointment of Agent form with a photocopy of their driver's license. Online, mail and self-service station options are not available for this title correction. Michigan estimates the corrected title arrives by mail within 14 days, with an optional extra $5 instant-title fee if eligible. Do not confuse correcting an owner's name with adding/removing another owner or a purchase transfer, which have distinct legal and tax consequences.

5. Re-register to vote using the married name

The Michigan Secretary of State states that a voter who has changed their legal name must re-register with updated information. Online registration at Michigan.gov/Vote and mail can generally be used 15 or more days before Election Day. Within 14 days of an election, update registration in person at your city/township clerk's office with any required proof of residency; election-day changes can be made through 8 p.m. if you are in line. For the November 3, 2026 election, the official state election calendar posted an October 19 last day for online/mail registration, with October 20–November 3 clerk-office in-person registration for the election. As of this review, use the appropriate in-person channel, and verify the clerk's location and identification evidence.

6. Determine whether to change a U.S. passport

Check the U.S. State Department's legal-name correction eligibility before choosing DS-5504, DS-82, or DS-11. The no-standard-application-fee DS-5504 process can apply if the passport was issued and the name changed during the qualifying past-year period; otherwise renewal or new-passport requirements apply. Bring or mail your certified marriage record, previous passport, photo and required fees according to the applicable form. Confirm travel dates before submitting the passport.

7. Complete employment, financial and other state records

After your identity documents match, update employer payroll/taxes, insurers, bank accounts, mortgage or property records, schools and professional credentials. If the filed Michigan marriage record itself contains a factual error, MDHHS directs applicants to contact the county clerk where the marriage license was issued for a record correction. A marriage record correction and an entirely new court-ordered name change are not the same procedure.

Michigan court-ordered name change when a certificate is insufficient

Where marriage evidence does not legally establish the desired change, Michigan's family division of circuit court handles petition-based name changes under MCL 711.1. Adult petitions generally require the petitioner to have resided in the county for at least one year and use state court Form PC 51. The form and MCR 3.613 describe publication procedures; Form PC 51c offers an ex-parte request for nonpublication and confidentiality when there is good cause involving harm, retaliation or discrimination. Age and background check requirements can apply. Filing charges vary by court and request, so verify with the clerk rather than treating the marriage-license or driver's-license fee as the court-petition fee.

Michigan preparation checklist

  1. Get the certified marriage certificate from the recorded county or MDHHS (state base fee $34).
  2. Change the name on your SSA record before your Michigan SOS appointment.
  3. Bring old/new legal-name evidence and your license/ID to the SOS; confirm $9 DL / $10 ID name-correction charges.
  4. Correct Michigan vehicle title, where applicable, with all owners/agent form and the $15 fee.
  5. Re-register under the new name with Michigan's online or in-person deadlines.
  6. Choose the appropriate passport application and update insurance, payroll and financial records.
