Louisiana name change after marriage: official process

Louisiana has a distinctive legal rule. Code of Civil Procedure article 3947(A) states that marriage does not itself change either spouse's legal name, but a married person may use the surname of either or both spouses. This guide describes agency record changes using a state-certified marriage document for an eligible married surname. Do not equate an automatically changed birth record with a lawful marital-surname use. A first-name change or another change not supported by the marriage document generally needs a separate court petition.

1. Obtain the certified marriage document from the correct parish or state office

In Louisiana, the issuing source depends on where the marriage license was recorded. If married in Orleans Parish, the Louisiana Department of Health Vital Records Registry issues certified Orleans marriage records. Order in person, by mail, or through its authorized VitalChek channel; the state's $5 Orleans Parish certified marriage certificate fee is listed before any special ordering charges. LDH’s mail-in instructions list approximately 8–10 weeks for mailed orders, so choose your route based on scheduling needs. A $0.50 statutory charge applies to mail-in or VitalChek orders under LDH’s service-fee schedule. For marriages recorded in other Louisiana parishes, contact the applicable Clerk of Court for certified marriage documents rather than assuming the Orleans-only state process applies. If married out of state, use the recording jurisdiction. For agencies requiring original or certified documents, an uncertified copy is insufficient.

2. Correct your Social Security name record first

Louisiana OMV checks name changes against the Social Security Administration electronically. Change your SSA record before visiting OMV; OMV policy currently advises that Social Security's database commonly takes approximately 24 hours to reflect a name update. Apply for a corrected card through SSA and submit original or government-certified evidence, proof of identity, and any immigration/citizenship documents needed for your case. SSA card corrections themselves are free. Do not assume filing an SSA application means OMV already has the revised record.

3. Replace your Louisiana driver’s license in person at OMV

After the SSA name update, appear at an OMV office with your current license, original or state-certified marriage document, proof of Louisiana residency, and insurance proof for any motor vehicle you own. The OMV FAQ says a vision test is required for this name-change transaction. Its currently published $17 duplicate six-year license charge can have an additional local fee not exceeding $8; a name-change duplicate does not extend the current license expiration date. If you also hold Louisiana ID or a mobility-impaired hangtag, OMV requires the name to be consistent across credentials and generally updates all affected valid credentials together. Confirm which fees apply to each credential during your appointment.

Louisiana OMV: required evidence, vision exam, and fees

Deadline safeguard: The OMV FAQ’s 10-day statutory period applies to reporting address changes, not necessarily a name change after marriage. Do not impose that address deadline on the married-name transaction without independent legal verification.

4. Review Louisiana vehicle-title and registration records

When a legal surname changes, ask OMV or your authorized title processing office whether a corrected certificate of title and/or vehicle registration is required for your specific vehicle and whether a lienholder must participate. Provide current title or registration, the certified marriage record, updated identification and proof of insurance if requested. OMV’s posted schedule lists $68.50 for a vehicle title correction and a possible $8 handling fee when that transaction is used. This schedule does not independently establish that every marriage requires purchasing a corrected physical vehicle title; confirm whether the title must be changed and what total charges apply. Adding or removing owners, or issuing a new title, can carry different taxes and document rules.

5. Update voter registration using Louisiana’s married-name rules

Louisiana Revised Statutes 18:111(B) explicitly permits a married voter to register with the surname of either spouse, both spouses, or a hyphenated combination. Submit a registration update with the desired allowed name, and your Louisiana driver-license number, special identification number, Social Security number, or the legally accepted attestation. Update online using the Secretary of State website (electronic submission normally requires a Louisiana DL or ID audit code) or use a parish Registrar of Voters paper/in-person method. 2026 election timing: the Secretary of State published an October 5, 2026 deadline for in-person/mail registration and October 13, 2026 deadline online for the November 3 election; both have passed as of this review. Contact your parish registrar for what voting procedures remain available rather than promising an overdue update.

6. Determine the appropriate U.S. passport correction form

U.S. Department of State requirements depend on when your current passport was issued and when your surname was adopted. Use DS-5504 if its passport-issuance and name-change timing requirements both apply; otherwise check DS-82 renewal eligibility or DS-11 in-person application rules. The passport office may require a certified marriage certificate, old passport, photo and fee depending on the form. Verify travel deadlines before surrendering your passport.

7. Finish employer, financial and professional records

Give each separate institution the name and certification documents it requires. This may include employer/payroll tax records, private insurance, bank accounts, real estate titles, business registrations, professional boards and airline loyalty profiles. The SSA and OMV name changes do not by themselves update these independent accounts.

Louisiana court-ordered name changes vs. married surname use

Louisiana's statutory authorization to use either or both spouses' surnames after marriage is separate from a judicial name change. Under R.S. 13:4751, an adult seeking a different name through the courts generally files a petition in district court in the parish of residence, parish of birth, or the parish where the Vital Records Registry is located. Court procedures and filing charges vary with the parish and case; obtain current requirements from the Clerk of Court. Do not represent a non-marital first-name change as automatically supported by a marriage certificate.

Louisiana preparation checklist

  1. Confirm the surname variation permitted by Louisiana art. 3947.
  2. Obtain a certified marriage certificate from Orleans Vital Records or the proper parish's Clerk of Court.
  3. Correct your Social Security record before OMV and allow verification time.
  4. Visit OMV with legal-name evidence, current license, residency and insurance; budget the posted license duplicate fees.
  5. Confirm whether title/registration or a lienholder record needs correction, and the applicable charge.
  6. Update Louisiana voter registration by the election deadline using the allowed marital surname.
  7. Choose the appropriate passport process and update employment, financial and insurance records.
