Maine marriage name change: state-specific legal and agency steps

Maine is one of the states where a proposed new name may be legally adopted through the marriage application itself. Title 19-A §§ 651, 654 and 656 govern the process. If the name you now want was not entered on the marriage intentions/license records, consult the issuing clerk and the receiving agency before assuming a certified certificate proves it. An unrelated name change may require a probate-court petition.

1. State the proposed new name on the marriage intentions application

Under 19-A M.R.S. §651(2), a couple filing the marriage intentions application must list a proposed new name for either party who intends to change that party's name. Section 654(3) requires the new name to appear in the completed marriage license, and section 656(3) states that the proposed name becomes legally effective on completion of the marriage license following the ceremony. Check spellings and name fields with the municipal clerk or State Registrar before the license is completed. Changing the name later to something different is not a routine correction to that original intent.

2. Obtain a certified recorded marriage certificate

The parties or their officiant must return the completed marriage license to the clerk who issued it or State Registrar within 15 working days after the ceremony, under the 2025-amended §654(2). This is a filing rule, not a deadline for the newly married person to correct all government IDs. After the record is accepted, order the government-certified marriage certificate from an authorized municipal office or Maine Division of Data, Research, and Vital Statistics (DRVS). The official state rate is $15 for the first certified record search and copy and $6 for each additional certified copy ordered at the same time; the fee may be charged even if the record is not found. An uncertified $10 copy is explicitly stamped not for legal purposes and is not an adequate substitute for the state credential update. DRVS accepts mail requests with ID and has an authorized online ordering partner with additional service fees. If married out of state, obtain the certified record from that jurisdiction.

3. Change the name on your Social Security record

Use the SSA name-correction process with the certified marriage record and accepted original/certified identification. Depending on eligibility, you may begin online or complete Form SS-5 and present evidence at SSA. A corrected Social Security card is free; ensure the legal surname matches before approaching other agencies to avoid verification mismatches. If your marriage record does not establish the intended name, SSA may ask for different legal evidence.

4. Notify Maine BMV within 30 days and obtain the corrected license or ID

29-A M.R.S. §1407 explicitly requires notice to Maine's Secretary of State/Bureau of Motor Vehicles within 30 days when a driver's-license or registration holder changes name. Unlike an address-only deadline, the statute expressly includes names. Notify BMV of the old and new names and license/registration numbers; then follow BMV's guidance for a corrected physical driver license or ID. Maine BMV accepts legal name-change evidence including a marriage certificate, divorce decree, or court document. If obtaining a REAL ID and the birth certificate or passport shows a different name, show each original or issuing-agency-certified document connecting the identity-document name to your present legal name, along with applicable proof of identity, Social Security number and two Maine residency documents.

Maine statutes treat a credential bearing updated information as a duplicate. BMV lists a $5 duplicate standard (non-REAL-ID) license/ID and $30 duplicate REAL ID license/ID. A standard duplicate rate does not mean every license-renewal or REAL ID upgrade costs $5. Confirm whether BMV can update the underlying name by correspondence or requires an office visit to inspect the certified marriage evidence. An online duplicate service is not necessarily a valid name-change submission.

5. Check the linked vehicle-registration and title records

Section 29-A §1407 includes license and registration holders in the 30-day notification requirement after a name change. When notifying BMV, identify any affected registration number. Because Maine's publicly reviewed guidance does not establish a universally mandatory replacement certificate of title for every same-owner married-name change, ask the Maine BMV title unit what specific document or transaction is appropriate and whether a lienholder must sign or supply the current title. The published $5 duplicate registration statutory fee applies when an updated or duplicate registration certificate is issued; do not assume it automatically covers title correction. Do not confuse a legal-name correction with adding a spouse as owner or transferring title.

6. Update Maine voter registration under §129

Under 21-A M.R.S. §129, a voter whose name changes by marriage must give the registrar notice of the old and new names or use the official online registration application so the central register is corrected. Maine's online and mail registration generally closes 21 days before Election Day; in-person voter registration can be available through Election Day. For November 3, 2026, the usual 21-day advance deadline was October 13, now passed. If voting soon, follow the municipal registrar's official in-person process instead of assuming a late mail or online submission updates the polling list in time.

7. Choose the correct U.S. passport form

The State Department's eligibility criteria determine whether to use DS-5504 (when both the current passport's issue date and legal name change satisfy the one-year rule), DS-82 renewal, or a DS-11 in-person application. Check photo, certified marriage-document submission, passport expiration and the processing fee requirements before arranging travel.

8. Update employers, insurers and private accounts

Update payroll/tax identity, medical and vehicle insurers, school records, banking institutions, professional registrations, mortgage or property records, and travel reservations as applicable. Keep certified originals safe and use direct official filing channels rather than uploading personal records into an unofficial website form.

When to petition Maine Probate Court for a separate name change

Maine's Title 18-C §1-701 provides a distinct process for an adult legal-name-change petition through probate court when the desired name is not established through marriage records. The statute lists a $75 petition filing fee and requires an adult criminal-history background check, with possible related costs. A petitioner may request confidentiality under the legal criteria, and the state does not generally require public notice. The court's order and its effective date are separate from obtaining a corrected married-name credential; confirm the probate-court procedure and current filing forms rather than paying the petition fee for an ordinary certified marriage-based update.

Maine preparation checklist

  1. Verify the proposed new name on the marriage intentions and license documents before solemnization.
  2. Get a government-certified recorded marriage certificate; budget $15 plus $6 additional certified copies as applicable.
  3. Update SSA with certified name-change evidence.
  4. Notify Maine BMV of the name change within 30 days and correct your license/ID; check whether $5 or $30 duplicate applies.
  5. Include affected registrations in the BMV name notice; confirm any vehicle-title requirements with the title unit.
  6. Update Maine voter registration with former/new names and applicable deadline.
  7. Choose the correct passport form; update employment and financial records separately.
