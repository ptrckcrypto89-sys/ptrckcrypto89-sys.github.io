New York marriage name change: certified state and city process

New York State and New York City do not use the same marriage-record office. For a marriage license purchased outside New York City, use the original town/city clerk or the New York State Department of Health. For a marriage license issued in Manhattan, Brooklyn, Queens, Bronx or Staten Island, use the New York City Clerk, not the state DOH. New York's marriage application allows specified surname and middle-name options; a certified certificate showing that choice generally establishes the married name without a separate court order.

1. Obtain the correct certified marriage certificate

Outside NYC, request a certified copy from the town or city clerk who issued the marriage license, or order through the NYS Department of Health Vital Records. The official NYS marriage guide identifies a $10 town/city clerk record fee while DOH now posts $30 by mail or $45 online/telephone plus a vendor processing fee. Clerk costs should be confirmed before ordering. For an NYC-issued marriage, the City Clerk charges $15 for the first domestic-use certificate and $10 for each additional domestic-use copy on the same request. City Clerk record requests require an appointment, which can be scheduled via the City's system; older NYC records have distinct collection locations. If married outside New York, order from the jurisdiction that registered that marriage.

2. Confirm the legally chosen surname or middle-name combination

Under the New York Department of Health's marriage rules, either spouse may elect certain surnames on the marriage license: the spouse's surname, certain former surnames, and specified combined or hyphenated options. A middle name can also be changed using legally specified surname choices. The recorded marriage certificate provides legal evidence of that choice. The original marriage license cannot be retroactively altered simply because someone decides to use a different surname after the wedding. If the desired first-name or surname change is unsupported by marriage evidence, check New York's adult court name-change procedure instead.

3. Request a free Social Security name correction

Update the Social Security Administration name record using your original or issuing-authority-certified marriage certificate and applicable identity, citizenship or lawful-status documentation. The SSA legal-name-change transaction is free. Some applicants can start online; others must submit documents or visit SSA. For New York DMV's new MV-44NC mail-only STANDARD-credential correction, your new name on your Social Security card must exactly match the name requested on the replacement NYS credential. Resolve mismatches before filing this route.

4. Update your New York driver license, permit or non-driver ID

Standard NYS driver licenses, permits and non-driver IDs: The DMV released Form MV-44NC (February 2026) specifically for a name change only. The form permits a mail submission with a copy of the existing photo credential, the corrected Social Security card, government-issued marriage certificate or other accepted legal-name evidence, signature and required payment. REAL ID, Enhanced and commercial licenses are excluded from this mail form; follow the applicable DMV office document-check and identity-chain procedure instead. REAL ID or Enhanced applicants may need certified records linking each prior name change.

The currently posted NYS fee to amend information on a driver license or learner permit is $12.50. The $17.50 replacement fee is a different transaction (for replacement of an otherwise unchanged driver credential). Non-driver ID and combined transactions may carry other fees. The official instructions for marriage-name correction do not state the same 10-day deadline as the separate NY address change law; do not conflate them. Update the name promptly to avoid mismatched documents.

5. Correct New York vehicle title, registration and insurance

New York DMV instructs registered vehicle owners to change title or registration information using Vehicle Registration/Title Application MV-82 and the instructions in MV-82.1. To correct your same-owner legal name, submit the existing Certificate of Title (MV-999) for post-1972 vehicles, identity proof, certified marriage proof, vehicle insurance matching the proposed registrant name and other applicable documentation. Name changes on driver licenses, registrations and titles are not automatically one transaction; MV-44NC expressly updates the photo credential only. Confirm the fees for the specific corrected documents at the DMV: the ordinary $50 first title certificate fee is not automatically the charge for a name-correction transaction. Do not treat a pure name correction as a vehicle sale.

6. Update New York voter registration before the general election

New York State Board of Elections permits voters to change name or address via the state online voter registration portal or registration form. For the November 3, 2026 General Election, the official voter registration cutoff was October 24, 2026 (applications needed to be received by the election board). For already-registered voters, NY law specifies that name changes may be submitted to the local county Board of Elections and that address change notices received at least 15 days before the election must be entered for that election. If your name changed after the registration deadline, contact the county board directly to confirm what identification, pollbook annotation or affidavit-ballot process is appropriate; do not assume that New York offers election-day new voter registration.

7. Review federal passport and remaining accounts

If you need a passport under the married name, use State Department guidance to select DS-5504 when its recent-issuance and name-change requirements are satisfied, or DS-82/DS-11 as appropriate. Submit the certified marriage record, previous passport, photo and payment where required. Separately update payroll, bank accounts, property records, insurance and professional licenses once your agency records match.

When a New York court name-change order is needed

New York law recognizes a legal surname chosen and documented at marriage, but a name not properly established through the marriage certificate may require a court petition. The New York State Unified Court System provides official adult name-change forms for petitioning Supreme Court or New York City Civil Court as applicable. The court self-help form program is for adult New York State residents age 18+ who are not currently incarcerated, on probation or on parole; those circumstances have different procedures. Court filings can require fees, identity evidence and possible publication/confidentiality decisions. A court order is not part of the routine process when a legally effective marriage record is sufficient.

New York preparation checklist

  1. Order an official marriage certificate from the city/town clerk outside NYC or NYC Clerk for five-borough marriages; verify the selected legal name.
  2. Submit the no-fee Social Security name correction and get documentary proof if using mail-in DMV MV-44NC.
  3. Amend a STANDARD NY license using MV-44NC where eligible or visit DMV for REAL ID/Enhanced/CDL; note the $12.50 license amendment fee.
  4. Correct registration and title separately via MV-82 and current same-owner legal-name evidence.
  5. Update voter registration; the November 3, 2026 statewide registration cutoff was October 24.
  6. Review U.S. passport form eligibility and update employer, insurer and financial records.
