Massachusetts marriage name change: state agency instructions

Massachusetts explicitly allows a person getting married to adopt any surname through the marriage process under MGL chapter 46, §1D, including the present/birth surname of either party or a hyphenated combination. Its courts explain that a separate court petition is not required solely to adopt a surname upon marriage. Changes not documented by the marriage event—such as an unrelated given-name change—may need judicial documentation.

1. Establish the surname during the Massachusetts marriage-record process

When filing the notice of intention of marriage with the city or town clerk, identify the surname you intend to use following the marriage. Massachusetts law does not limit the choice to taking the other spouse's surname; chapter 46, §1D permits either party to adopt any surname as part of marriage. Review spelling and the surname fields with the municipal clerk before the license and marriage certificate are finalized. Once filed, obtain the certified record that connects the before-marriage identity to the chosen post-marriage surname for other government agencies.

Massachusetts legal rule allowing any married surname · Massachusetts court explanation: marriage requires no name-change petition

2. Obtain an agency-certified Massachusetts marriage certificate

Order a certificate from the city or town clerk that holds the record or Massachusetts Registry of Vital Records and Statistics (RVRS). Statewide RVRS certified-copy pricing varies substantially by channel: $20 in person, $32 by standard mail, $42 expedited by mail, and $54 for the first standard online/telephone copy (including the vendor's processing fee), with higher charges for expedited vendor service. Municipal clerk fees can differ, so compare official channels before paying. RVRS requires information such as the spouses' names, date and place of marriage and proof of identification or eligibility. A certified state/local record is different from an unofficial printed ceremonial certificate.

3. Correct the Social Security name record before identity-matching at RMV

Use the Social Security Administration's corrected-card process with the marriage record and required proof of identity. Some eligible applicants can start online; other applications may require Form SS-5 or an appointment. Submit only original or issuing-agency-certified evidence as SSA requires; name corrections are free. The SSA database and new license should represent the same legal surname to prevent mismatched payroll and benefits records. No exact Massachusetts-specific waiting period between SSA and RMV has been verified here, so confirm processing status rather than inventing a fixed number of hours.

4. Correct the Massachusetts driver’s license or ID in person at RMV

Massachusetts RMV requires a service-center appointment to change the name on a license or ID, where a new photograph and signature may be captured; complete the required application and bring it to the appointment. The state makes a significant distinction between credentials: a REAL ID applicant whose current name differs from lawful-presence source documents must produce legal-name-change evidence, such as a certified marriage certificate, divorce decree or court order. For a Standard (non-REAL-ID) license/ID, RMV says a name change may be permitted without that legal-document evidence as long as it is not fraudulent. A replacement-card fee applies, but the currently reviewed name-change page does not clearly assign a unique name-change transaction fee; check RMV's fee schedule at the appointment rather than treating an address-replacement fee as definitive for a name correction.

5. Amend a vehicle title, and registration if needed, through RMV

When the owner's name on a Massachusetts vehicle title is no longer accurate, the RMV directs an amended certificate-of-title transaction. Provide the original title (no copies), completed title amendment form signed by every owner, the marriage evidence or other relevant name-change documentation, and the $25 title amendment fee. If a lienholder holds the original, follow RMV's lienholder procedures; a new title may be mailed to the lienholder. If the vehicle has an active registration, confirm with your insurer whether an updated stamped Registration and Title Application is necessary. Adding or removing a vehicle owner is a different transaction—not a mere title correction—and can carry additional obligations.

6. Update voter registration before the deadline

The Massachusetts Secretary of the Commonwealth advises voters to update registration after a legal name change, and allows updating online or through local election channels. Voter registration and updates normally must be submitted at least 10 days before an election. For the November 3, 2026 election, the normal 10-day deadline was October 24, 2026, which has already passed as of this review. Contact the local election office to confirm identity/voting procedures if the existing registration still reflects your former name rather than asserting a missed deadline automatically removes your voting eligibility.

7. Correct the U.S. passport if needed

The State Department's requirements depend on when you obtained your passport and when you adopted your legal surname. DS-5504 can apply when both the passport issuance and legal name change meet its one-year conditions, otherwise the proper route may be DS-82 or DS-11. Verify the application fee, certified marriage evidence, passport and photo requirements for the form chosen before travel.

8. Update payroll, insurance and other private records

Update your employer's tax and payroll system, insurance plans, banks, financial providers, educational credentials, licensing boards, property records and travel accounts separately. Avoid sharing identity documents with unaffiliated services; a checklist does not file requests or update accounts automatically.

Massachusetts court petition is separate from adopting a married surname

The Massachusetts Trial Court explicitly states that marriage itself is a recognized legal name-change pathway and no probate petition should be filed just to change a surname upon marriage. For an adult seeking a change not supported by the marriage record, the Probate and Family Court accepts Form CJP-27, signed before a notary, with certified civil records and other required supporting forms. Current posted petition filing is $165 ($150 plus a $15 surcharge), with a possible extra $15 citation and e-filing charges. This court route uses rules updated effective January 6, 2026. Under a 2025 statutory amendment, post-November 25, 2025 name-petition case materials are generally shielded from routine public inspection absent a court order; consult the court's confidentiality guidance for your case.

Massachusetts preparation checklist

  1. Record the desired surname with the city/town clerk during the marriage-intentions process.
  2. Order the certified marriage record, checking local clerk vs RVRS fee by ordering channel.
  3. Correct the Social Security record with certified marriage evidence.
  4. Book the in-person Massachusetts RMV name-correction visit, bringing REAL ID documentation where necessary.
  5. If you own a vehicle, request amended title with original title, signed amendment form and the $25 fee.
  6. Update voter registration before the normal 10-day pre-election cutoff.
  7. Choose the applicable passport form and separately update private accounts and payroll.
