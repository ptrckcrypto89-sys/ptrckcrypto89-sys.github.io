Ohio marriage name change: certified documents and agency order

Ohio marriage certificate copies come from the county probate court, not Ohio Vital Statistics. If your name has changed, Ohio BMV requires an original or government-certified marriage certificate/license (or other legal proof) linking the original birth/passport name to the current name. Ohio's election office requires a physical voter-registration name update, not an online name update; its Form 10-L provides an important safeguard for already-registered voters whose names changed before an election.

1. Get an official marriage record from the correct Ohio probate court

Contact the probate court in the county where your Ohio marriage was recorded. Ohio Department of Health Vital Statistics does not maintain or issue certified marriage records. Each probate court sets its certified copy process and fees. Ask for a certified marriage record with an official seal that provides the old and new legal-name link needed for identity changes. If your marriage was registered outside Ohio, obtain an issuing-authority-certified document from that jurisdiction.

Ohio Department of Health: probate courts hold marriage records

2. Update Social Security before visiting the BMV

Apply to the Social Security Administration to change the name on your federal earnings record and Social Security card. Bring or submit the proper issuing-agency-certified marriage evidence and identity/citizenship or lawful-status documents. SSA name corrections are free. A corrected SSA record can help avoid a mismatch when Ohio BMV verifies identification; Ohio's BMV may request more evidence of intervening marriages/divorces if the name shown on your primary ID is different from your current legal name.

3. Replace your Ohio driver license or non-driver ID under the new name

Visit an Ohio BMV deputy registrar and present the original or certified marriage certificate/license, existing Ohio credential, and any proof of full legal-name chain required to connect your current identity with your birth certificate or passport. The BMV's standard and compliant REAL ID checklists may require additional residency, Social Security or lawful-status records. Multiple prior marriages may require a certified document for each intervening legal name change.

The BMV's current statewide fee list (updated September 30, 2025) gives $29 for a duplicate operator driver license, $31.50 for a duplicate CDL, and $12 for a duplicate standard state ID. These are listed duplicate-document rates; the driver's-license name correction typically calls for reissuance, but verify eligibility for any fee exemption or different credential transaction at the deputy registrar. Ohio BMV's reviewed name-change guidance does not establish the same universally applicable 10- or 30-day name-update window found in certain other states; complete it promptly rather than importing another state's deadline.

4. Correct the Ohio vehicle certificate of title

Ohio BMV directs owners to any County Clerk of Courts Title Office for a replacement title when information on the previously issued title must change. Take current proof of identity under the new name, legal-name-change evidence and existing title/title number or other documents the title office requires. For an electronic title with a lien, contact the lienholder before altering the owner's title name. The standard title fee is $18 statewide, rising to $23 in counties that have adopted a local additional title fee; confirm the correct replacement/correction amount with your county clerk before paying. Do not file a sale or transfer of ownership when only the same person's legal name changed.

5. Update Ohio voter registration before the November 2026 election

The Ohio Secretary of State instructs people changing their legal name to submit a signed physical voter-registration update; legal-name updates cannot be completed online under its current election FAQ. For November 3, 2026, the ordinary voter-registration/update deadline was October 5, 2026, and early voting began October 6. If you are already registered in the same precinct but changed your name after the cutoff, you can sign Form 10-L at the polling place and show your marriage license or court order displaying the former/new names to cast a regular ballot. When the documented name-change proof is unavailable, a provisional ballot may be required. Changing addresses across precincts has different requirements; consult the county elections office.

6. Change passport name if needed

The U.S. Department of State uses different forms based on the issuance date, when the name changed, and renewal eligibility. A qualifying recent change may permit DS-5504, while others use DS-82 or DS-11. Present the certified marriage document, existing passport, photograph and relevant fees in accordance with the selected form. Check travel booking details first.

7. Finish private and non-DMV name updates

Correct employer payroll and tax files, bank/credit accounts, insurance policies, vehicle coverage, property records, professional licenses, health records and school files. Retain the official certified marriage record when a new government identification card is not by itself sufficient to link your former name.

Ohio court name change instead of married-name evidence

A documented marriage-based surname change typically does not require a separate court order. For an unrelated first, middle or last name change not established by the marriage record, Ohio offers an adult name-change application in county Probate Court under Ohio Revised Code Chapter 2717. The Ohio Supreme Court's Form 21.0 is the application; Form 21.01 is a supporting affidavit. Applicants generally must have lived in the county at least 60 days immediately before filing. Additional criminal-history screening, service and possible publication requirements vary with circumstances. Court fees differ by county; check the applicable probate court requirements before filing.

Ohio preparation checklist

  1. Order the certified marriage record from the issuing Ohio county probate court, not the state health office.
  2. Use SSA's free legal-name update and collect certified documents connecting previous and new legal names.
  3. Apply at a BMV deputy registrar for a corrected Ohio license/ID, budgeting for the applicable duplicate-card fee.
  4. Correct motor-vehicle title name at an Ohio County Clerk of Courts Title Office and coordinate any lienholder.
  5. File a signed voter name update; if already registered and too late, review Ohio election Form 10-L procedures.
  6. Correct U.S. passport and payroll, insurance and finance records.
