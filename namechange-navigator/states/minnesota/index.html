Minnesota marriage name change: complete government-agency process

A marriage record ordinarily documents a married surname change when the chosen name is supported by that record. Minnesota also offers a separate adult district-court name-change petition for names that cannot be established using marriage evidence. Each agency independently evaluates acceptable name evidence; a checklist alone is not a legal filing.

Special Minnesota marriage-name restriction: Minnesota Statutes § 517.08 states that a party with a felony conviction under Minnesota, another state, or federal law cannot change their name through the marriage-license application process and must follow the court procedure in § 259.13. The ordinary marriage-certificate route should not be represented as universally available. See official marriage statute and special name-change statute.

1. Request the certified marriage certificate from the issuing county

The Minnesota Department of Health does not record marriages or issue certified marriage certificates. Obtain yours from the county office that issued the marriage license, rather than ordering a marriage certificate from the state health department. The Minnesota Official Marriage System (MOMS) helps locate marriage records and the responsible county. Fees for certified copies vary by county; there is no single statewide MDH fee for a Minnesota marriage certificate. A ceremonial certificate, scan, or ordinary photocopy may not satisfy an identity agency. When married outside Minnesota, use the issuing jurisdiction's official registrar.

2. Correct your name with the Social Security Administration

Request a corrected Social Security card directly through SSA, using an original or issuing-agency-certified marriage record and identity/lawful-status evidence appropriate to your circumstances. Some applicants can start online; others need an appointment or Form SS-5. SSA name corrections are free. Check that the SSA record reflects your intended name before attempting Minnesota driver licensing to avoid identity-matching failures; the state legal deadline for the license still applies.

3. Apply for a corrected Minnesota driver's license within 30 days

Minnesota Statutes §171.11 expressly requires a driver who changes their name by marriage or otherwise to apply for a duplicate driver's license within 30 days. This is a name-change deadline, not just an address deadline. Minnesota Administrative Rule 7410.0500 describes documentary evidence for changes and requires presentation of a qualifying prior credential and proof of the name change, such as a government-certified marriage record. Apply through an authorized Minnesota Driver and Vehicle Services (DVS) office, exam station, or participating deputy registrar that accepts corrected credential applications; bring existing license, certified marriage document, and any ID/residency evidence the location requires.

30-day deadline: Minnesota §171.11 · Minnesota Rule 7410.0500: required evidence · County license offices processing name-change duplicates

The state's posted duplicate driver's license fee is $26 for standard or REAL ID; an enhanced driver's license duplicate adds $15 for a total of $41, not including payment-processing charges where applicable. These are replacement-card rates, not normal renewals or first-time issuance fees. For a new REAL ID, Minnesota DVS requires original or agency-certified documents creating a complete chain between the underlying birth/passport name and your current married name; photocopies, scans and images on a phone are not accepted.

4. Correct a Minnesota vehicle title using DVS Form PS2025A

If a Minnesota certificate of title bears your former legal surname, DVS's title processing booklet specifically instructs completing the Application for Corrected Title/Odometer (PS2025A) for a legal-name correction. Only one owner must sign for this transaction. Enter the updated Minnesota license or ID number or provide government-certified marriage evidence if the credential has not yet been updated. If a lien is already recorded, a lien release is not required merely for the name correction; the replacement title will still show the lien. If releasing the lien as a separate transaction, complete the correction first. Ask a DVS deputy registrar for the corrected-title charge, as the reviewed processing guide does not establish a universal final total. Do not treat this as a sale or spouse ownership transfer.

Minnesota DVS title-processing booklet: legal-name correction

5. Re-register to vote or update the existing Minnesota voter record

The Minnesota Secretary of State instructs voters to update their registration when their name changes. Online or mail preregistration ordinarily closes about 20 days before an election; the official Minnesota online registration website currently states that preregistration is closed for the November 3, 2026 election and will reopen November 4. You can still register or update at the polling place on Election Day with the required Minnesota identity and residence evidence. Crucially, the Secretary of State says an otherwise registered voter at the same precinct who changed their name can notify the election judge of the prior name so they can locate the registration record. If you moved, separate residency rules also apply.

6. Update or correct a federal U.S. passport

Follow the U.S. Department of State's marriage-name correction process based on the issue date of your current passport and the date your name changed. DS-5504 may apply if both meet the qualifying one-year conditions; otherwise check eligibility for DS-82 renewal or DS-11 in-person application. Instructions differ on passport submissions, photos, certified documents and fees. Check your upcoming travel dates before sending your passport.

State Department passport name-change instructions

7. Update work, insurance and other financial records

After your government identity documents are corrected, separately update employer payroll and tax information, health and auto insurance, banks and lenders, medical providers, professional licenses, schools, real-property records, and travel accounts. These institutions may request different documentation; protect originals and obtain additional certified copies only where needed.

When Minnesota requires a separate court-order name change

For a change not demonstrated by the marriage record, Minnesota Statutes Chapter 259 provides a district-court name-change process, usually for adults who have resided in Minnesota for at least six months. The Minnesota Judicial Branch provides the adult Application for Name Change (NAM102), supporting criminal-history release form and filing procedures. Generally two adult witnesses must appear at the hearing to establish identity; court-approved exceptions may apply. Additional publication or land-record requirements depend on the case. Fees differ by county and special circumstances and are not the same as the $26 DVS replacement-license fee. Verify the current court forms and fee with the district court before filing.

Minnesota preparation checklist

  1. Find the county that issued the marriage license and order its certified recorded marriage certificate.
  2. Correct your SSA name record using government-certified evidence.
  3. Apply for a duplicate Minnesota driver's license within 30 days of marriage-related name change; budget the $26 standard/REAL ID duplicate fee.
  4. Bring each certified name-change link if applying for a REAL ID with a different name on base identity evidence.
  5. Correct a vehicle title using DVS PS2025A when applicable; preserve existing lien unless separately releasing it.
  6. Update Minnesota voter registration or notify the Election Day judge of the prior registered name, using official eligibility rules.
  7. Choose the correct passport form; update payroll, financial accounts, property and insurance records.
