North Dakota marriage name change: state and federal procedure

North Dakota's driver's-license name update is due within 10 days. The North Dakota DOT says people whose legal names change must visit an NDDOT Driver License Site and purchase a corrected license within that period. North Dakota is also the only state without voter registration; changing your name before voting means checking the name and residential address on an acceptable voting ID, not submitting a voter-registration application.

1. Order your marriage record from the county that issued the license

North Dakota Health and Human Services states that all certified copies of marriage records must be issued by the county where the marriage license was originally purchased and filed. Contact that county's designated clerk/recorder, since offices and certified copy fees vary by county. Do not request a full certified marriage certificate from the state vital-record office. A souvenir certificate or scanned photo is not equivalent to an official certified record. For an out-of-state marriage, contact the recording authority in the other state.

2. Change Social Security to match the married name

Use the SSA's legal-name-change process to request a corrected Social Security card, supplying the original or certified marriage document and appropriate identity, citizenship or lawful-status evidence. An SSA name correction is free; applications may start online for some eligible people. Update the record early so employer and federal identity files match, but do not overlook North Dakota's separate 10-day DOT credential requirement. For REAL ID cards, NDDOT may request evidence linking the name shown on each of your identity documents.

3. Correct the ND driver license or ID within 10 days

The current North Dakota DOT driver-license FAQ says anyone with a legal name change must visit a Driver License Site and purchase a corrected credential within 10 days. Take the existing license or ID and a government-certified marriage certificate, court order or appropriate adoption document, along with any name-chain and REAL ID evidence. The NDDOT fee schedule lists a $3 duplicate driver license or permit for a name or address change only (versus $8 for a standard lost/stolen duplicate). These are specific transaction fees and do not replace other full-license/REAL ID fees that might apply in a different service scenario.

4. Update the owner name on North Dakota vehicle title/registration

If an ND car, truck or other titled vehicle is registered under your previous name, schedule an NDDOT Motor Vehicle name-change service. The agency's What to Bring checklist requires the North Dakota title, current vehicle mileage, and a valid state/government photo ID showing your new legal name. Check whether the title or record is electronic/held by a lender and ask about any extra name-link evidence. Application SFN 2872 is the NDDOT Certificate of Title and Registration form for applicable vehicle transactions. Fees can depend on whether a corrected title or duplicate is issued; do not mistake a legal-name correction for a transfer between owners. NDDOT's SFN 2872 schedule shows $5 for duplicate title issuance; confirm if this amount applies to the exact corrected-title transaction.

5. Verify acceptable voting identification—ND does not register voters

The North Dakota Secretary of State says no state voter registration exists. Eligible voters normally must be U.S. citizens, age 18 or older, and North Dakota residents for at least 30 days before Election Day. Voting identity relies on a qualifying North Dakota driver license, North Dakota non-driver ID, tribal ID/letter or long-term-care certificate. Check that your legal-name and residential-address records are aligned after marriage before November 3, 2026; NDDOT ID data are synchronized with the election office's Central Voter File. The state has separate procedures when the ID name or address differs from the voting record; review the Secretary of State's current specific identification and supplemental-document options. Do not send an ordinary voter registration form—there is none.

6. Correct a U.S. passport if needed

The State Department determines the correct passport name-change form based on the issuance and legal change dates and your renewal eligibility. Certain recent holders may qualify to file DS-5504 during its one-year window, while others use DS-82 or DS-11. Supply the certified marriage record, old passport, photograph and applicable payment according to the selected form. Confirm travel reservations and deadlines before surrendering the passport.

7. Update banking, employers, insurers and other records

Update employment/payroll and tax identity records, bank accounts, insurance plans, property records, professional registrations, schools and medical providers. Retain the certified marriage certificate as documentary evidence of the change.

When a North Dakota court order is needed

A certified marriage certificate generally establishes an appropriate married-name choice for agency purposes. If you want a different legal name not supported by marriage evidence, the North Dakota district court provides an adult name-change petition. North Dakota Courts reports an applicant must generally have lived in the same North Dakota county for at least six months and be a U.S. citizen or permanent resident. Newspaper notice is normally published in that county 30 days before the court considers the petition, although the court can waive publication for certain first/middle-name or domestic-violence circumstances. Filing and publication fees are separate. Court self-help forms are not guaranteed to be accepted in all cases and applicants should check the current court requirements.

North Dakota preparation checklist

  1. Order a government-certified marriage certificate from the county that issued the marriage license.
  2. Request SSA name correction using certified evidence (no SSA fee).
  3. Apply in person for a new North Dakota license/ID within 10 days; the posted name-only duplicate license fee is $3.
  4. Correct motor-vehicle title/registration records, taking the title, updated ID and mileage to the NDDOT office.
  5. Confirm acceptable voting ID name/address information; North Dakota has no voter-registration process.
  6. Choose the correct U.S. passport form and update employers, financial accounts and insurers.
