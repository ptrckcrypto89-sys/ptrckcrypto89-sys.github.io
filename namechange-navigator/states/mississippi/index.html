Mississippi marriage name change: official step-by-step guidance

Mississippi's Department of Public Safety accepts an official marriage license as name-change evidence for a driver's license or state ID. A separate chancery-court name-change petition may be needed for changes unsupported by the marriage record, such as an unrelated first-name change. A marriage does not automatically correct each government or vehicle record.

1. Obtain a government-certified Mississippi marriage record

Mississippi State Department of Health (MSDH) maintains marriage records for January 1, 1926 through June 30, 1938, and January 1, 1942 to the present. For marriages July 1, 1938–December 31, 1941 contact the circuit court clerk in the county that issued the license. You may also contact the issuing county clerk for later records. State certification requires the couples' names, marriage date and county if known, eligibility and a valid photo ID. MSDH currently charges $17 for one certified search/copy and $6 per extra certified copy ordered with it. The initial search fee is generally nonrefundable, including when a record is not found. For mail orders send the proper form and a cashier's check or money order, not cash or a personal check. Authorized online/telephone services can add processing fees. A government-certified record differs from an uncertified ceremonial printout.

2. Correct your Social Security name record, understanding Mississippi's exception

SSA accepts a marriage certificate or other qualifying issuing-agency-certified name-change evidence with proper identification; name corrections are free. Update SSA soon after receiving your certified record to align your payroll, tax and government identity records. Mississippi Driver Service Bureau's current Required Documents page specifically states that the Social Security card does not have to show the new name at the initial driver-license name-change transaction, although it strongly recommends updating SSA before renewing. This is not the same as the mandatory SSA-first matching requirements of certain other states. Have an accepted Social Security number/document and ensure the name can be verified as directed by DPS.

3. Change Mississippi driver's license or ID through Driver Service Bureau

Visit a Mississippi Driver Service Bureau station to correct the name on a driver license or ID. DPS limits acceptable legal name evidence to marriage license, divorce decree, adoption order, or court order. Bring the original or government-certified document plus your prior credential and required proof of identity/SSN and residency where requested. DPS's standard duplicate driver's license or ID fee is $11, as listed in its published fees. A station visit requires a new photograph and duplicate application; the online duplicate-replacement service is not evidence that an unverified marriage-name change can be processed completely online. DPS recommends an appointment. The reviewed Mississippi DPS name-change FAQ does not give a fixed number of days after marriage to complete the correction; verify if a separate statute or particular credential type imposes a deadline rather than borrowing an address-change period.

4. Correct a Mississippi vehicle title at the county Tax Collector

The Mississippi Department of Revenue expressly addresses changing the current title owner's name due to marriage or court order. Bring the existing certificate of title to your county Tax Collector's office and submit a copy of the marriage license or court order. Be clear that this is a same-owner name update, not adding a new nonfamily owner or transferring a sold vehicle (which may involve tax and other fees). Mississippi DOR lists $9 for a standard title and $39 for expedited Fast Track title processing. Its public FAQ lists $10 for correcting an error caused by submitted title paperwork, and no charge for a state/county clerical error. The public FAQ does not unambiguously assign those error rates to each marriage-name correction; ask the Tax Collector to confirm the appropriate charge and whether registration/plate records need adjustment.

5. Update the voter registration with the Secretary of State

The Mississippi Secretary of State offers voters an application to change name and/or address; update through the county Circuit Clerk, an official voter-registration form, or YallVote.ms where you meet its identity requirements. The state generally requires a form to be received or postmarked at least 30 days before an election, with extensions if the cutoff falls on a Sunday or legal holiday. For the November 3, 2026 general election, the Secretary of State expressly published October 5, 2026 as the registration deadline, which has passed. If the registration still shows a previous name, consult the Circuit Clerk about the valid photo-ID and voting options for your record; do not promise a late online update will be accepted for that election.

6. Decide whether and how to correct your U.S. passport

For a passport, the U.S. Department of State's process depends on both the passport issuance date and the date your legal name changed. Form DS-5504 can apply when both timing conditions fit the one-year rule. Otherwise review renewal by mail using DS-82 or new in-person application DS-11. The appropriate government-certified marriage document, previous passport, photo, and payment requirements differ by method. Verify travel dates before sending the current passport.

7. Complete payroll, business, insurance and other account updates

Update payroll and tax withholding records, banks, mortgages, professional licensing, car insurers, health providers, real estate records and airline profiles separately. Agencies may require original or certified evidence and will not all update when DPS updates your license. Order certified copies only as required and keep government identity documents secure.

Mississippi court name change outside ordinary marriage evidence

If the desired change is not supported by a marriage license, a separate legal name-change case may need to be filed with the Chancery Court in your county of residence under Mississippi Code §93-17-1. The court's forms, petition notarization, notice/hearing requirements, evidence, and filing charge must be checked with the county Chancery Clerk. The legal petition process is not a duplicate-driver's-license transaction, and its fees can vary by county. A later request to amend a birth record pursuant to a court order may be a further process with MSDH. Do not assume a routine married-surname update requires this court petition.

Mississippi preparation checklist

  1. Obtain a certified marriage record from MSDH or the licensing county; check the 1938–1941 records exception.
  2. Correct SSA records using a certified marriage record; note Mississippi DPS's initial-license-card exception.
  3. Visit a Driver Service Bureau station with accepted marriage evidence, prior license/ID and the $11 duplicate fee.
  4. Visit the county Tax Collector to update a vehicle title with a marriage-license copy if applicable.
  5. Update registration through the county Circuit Clerk or eligible YallVote process before the cutoff.
  6. Choose the appropriate federal passport form and separately update employment, insurance and financial accounts.
