Hawaii marriage name change: step-by-step guide

This guide covers a marriage-based name change reflected in official documentation. For a Hawaii resident married in Hawaii who declared on the marriage certificate that they will take their spouse's surname, Hawaii's Lieutenant Governor says a separate name-change petition is not necessary. A different desired name, a record that does not establish the change, or another legal situation may require the separate petition process.

1. Confirm whether your marriage certificate establishes your new surname

Do not pay for a separate court or Lieutenant Governor name-change proceeding unnecessarily. Hawaii's Lieutenant Governor expressly exempts a Hawaii resident married in Hawaii who declared their spouse's surname on the marriage certificate. If you did not make that declaration, or the certificate does not document your requested name, review the state's separate name-change instructions. They list a nonrefundable $50 filing fee, $1 archive charge and $5 online service fee, plus publication and recording fees; this is a separate process, not a standard requirement for a qualifying marital surname change.

Hawaii Lieutenant Governor — when a separate petition is or is not necessary · Official Hawaii name-change application portal

2. Order your certified marriage certificate

Order an official certified marriage record from the Hawaii Department of Health if the marriage occurred in Hawaii. A marriage license authorizes a ceremony; the marriage certificate confirms the marriage was performed. Eligible requesters may order online or by mail, with proof of identity. The state currently lists $10 for the first certified copy, $4 per additional copy and a $2.50 administrative fee for up to five copies. Processing may take several weeks; check current timelines. If the marriage took place elsewhere, request the record from the jurisdiction that registered the marriage.

Hawaii Department of Health — certified marriage copies, fees and eligibility · Hawaii DOH — difference between a marriage license and certificate

3. Correct your name in Social Security records

Request an updated Social Security record and replacement card from SSA. You may be eligible to begin online; others must present required documents at a local office. SSA recommends correcting your record before updating other identity documents to reduce matching problems. Retain your supporting certified marriage record and follow the SSA instructions for identity and name-change evidence.

Social Security Administration — correct your name

4. Update your Hawaii driver license in person within 30 days

Under Hawaii Revised Statutes § 286-116.5, a driver-license holder must notify the examiner of drivers of a legal name change within 30 days, providing former and new names and the license or permit number. Hawaii DOT says that when a name printed on a license changes, the person must present original or certified documentary proof in person at a county driver-licensing office. Present your marriage certificate or other connecting document and confirm the county's application, appointment, residence evidence and fee requirements. For example, Honolulu uses AlohaQ appointments and requires a driver's-license application; Hawaii County asks for the current license, certified name document and address evidence. Fees vary by county; do not assume statewide pricing.

Hawaii Revised Statutes § 286-116.5 — 30-day name-change notice · Hawaii DOT — legal-name and connecting-document requirements · Honolulu — changing a driver-license name · Hawaii County — name-change evidence and fees

5. Review your county's vehicle-title and registration records

Hawaii does not have a single statewide DMV for vehicle registration: the counties administer it. If your name appears on a vehicle title or registration, ask the registration office in your own county—Honolulu, Maui, Hawaii or Kauai—whether and how to update it after changing the legal name on your identification. Prepare certified marriage evidence, current title/registration and any lienholder documentation they require. Do not apply Honolulu's rules to other islands without checking.

Hawaii DOT — county-managed motor-vehicle registration · Honolulu — vehicle registration services

6. Correct Hawaii voter registration

The Hawaii Office of Elections requires an update when you legally change your name. You can update through the state's online voter-registration service (Hawaii driver license or state ID and Social Security number required) or submit a signed paper application to your County Elections Division. Applying for a new driver license or ID can also automatically update registration unless you decline. Check your record and relevant election deadlines; mail ballots are sent using registration details.

Hawaii Office of Elections — change your voter registration · Hawaii online voter-registration system

7. Update passport, employer and financial records

Use the U.S. Department of State instructions to determine whether a passport name change uses DS-5504, DS-82 or DS-11, based on when the passport was issued and when the name changed. Separately correct payroll, insurance, travel profiles, bank accounts, professional credentials and other records with each issuing organization. Hawaii preparation checklist

  1. Check whether your Hawaii marriage certificate records the new surname; avoid an unnecessary separate name-change petition.
  2. Order a certified marriage certificate from Hawaii DOH or the government where the marriage occurred.
  3. Correct Social Security records and retain the name-change evidence.
  4. Notify Hawaii's county examiner of drivers within 30 days; attend in person with original or certified connecting documents.
  5. Ask your county vehicle-registration office to update title/registration records when applicable.
  6. Update Hawaii voter registration online or through the county; check for automatic DMV changes.
  7. Determine the applicable passport form and correct private accounts and payroll records.
